# Registro de visuales

Los visuales explicativos son una capa editorial; nunca sustituyen datos, figuras primarias ni registros de evidencia. Cuando una pieza sea conceptual, su leyenda debe decirlo. Cuando represente relaciones científicas, debe poder reconstruirse desde código o texto y enlazar los claims pertinentes.

## Prompt de la portada

**Modo:** generación nueva, sin imagen de referencia.

> Create an original wide 16:9 cinematic editorial illustration for an open Spanish-language scientific research project titled conceptually “How do we know what we know? Deep history of Earth, life, and humanity.” No text, no labels, no logos, no watermark. One continuous left-to-right visual journey with smooth transitions: faint cosmic microwave background texture and cosmic web; stellar nucleosynthesis and a forming protoplanetary disk; a luminous molten early Earth with a small young Moon; cooling crust with a microscopic zircon crystal; an ancient ocean with subtle microbial mats; layered fossils and branching life; distant early human silhouettes observing the sky; the faint geometry of an early city at the far right. Scientifically grounded rather than fantasy, elegant dark indigo and charcoal background, mineral gold and cyan highlights, restrained color, high-detail natural-history museum aesthetic, dramatic but sober, ample dark negative space near upper center for a title to be added later. Do not show dinosaurs beside humans, do not imply literal scale or simultaneity, no religious symbols, no clocks, no equations, no typography.

La imagen se conserva como una metáfora temporal continua. Los elementos vecinos pertenecen a épocas distintas; esa compresión se declara para impedir una lectura literal.

A coherent left-to-right cosmic history in one museum-quality scene: at far left the hot early universe cooling into simple hydrogen and helium nuclei; then a luminous main-sequence star with a subtle cutaway showing nested fusion regions; next a red AGB giant releasing glowing dust and microscopic presolar grains; then an asymmetric core-collapse supernova with faint gamma-ray arcs; then two neutron stars merging into a blue-to-red kilonova; in the foreground a dark meteorite fragment, a single silicon-carbide grain under a laboratory ion probe, and a glass spectrograph prism splitting starlight into fine spectral lines. Sober deep navy, charcoal, amber, cyan and muted magenta palette; realistic scientific editorial illustration, intricate but uncluttered, strong visual flow, generous negative space near the upper left for a future title. La portada es conceptual. El aparente orden espacial solo guía lectura; no afirma simultaneidad, escala, anatomía estelar exacta ni genealogía exclusiva de núcleos.

## Prompt de la portada de la Investigación 004

**Modo:** generación nueva, sin imagen de referencia. Archivo original de salida conservado por ImageGen; copia final en `assets/visuales/hero-evolucion-estelar.png`.

> Create an original wide 16:9 museum-quality editorial science illustration for a rigorous public Spanish-language research project about how we reconstruct stellar birth, evolution, and death. No text, no labels, no letters, no numbers, no arrows, no logos, no watermark. Compose a visually coherent but clearly branching scene, not one universal linear lifecycle: at left a cold dark molecular cloud with an embedded protostar, a resolved circumstellar disk, and a narrow bipolar jet; toward center a stable sun-like star and a diverse coeval star cluster; from there the scene branches. One branch shows a swollen red giant shedding a luminous but delicate shell around a compact white dwarf. A separate branch shows a massive red supergiant and an asymmetric core-collapse supernova leading to a compact rotating neutron star with subtle lighthouse-like beams. In the far background, a massive blue companion orbits an invisible stellar-mass black-hole candidate with a restrained accretion glow, avoiding fantasy depictions of the event horizon. In the foreground integrate observational instruments as visual motifs: a parallax baseline against background stars, a glass spectrograph splitting starlight, an eclipsing binary light curve suggested only as luminous geometry without written symbols, and a sparse color–magnitude population pattern rendered as points rather than a labeled chart. Scientifically sober, deep navy and charcoal, cold molecular blues, amber stellar light, muted crimson supernova, cyan instrument accents, high detail, elegant natural-history museum aesthetic, ample dark negative space near upper left for a future title. Avoid showing exact mass thresholds, avoid implying all stars pass through every object, avoid presenting the black hole as directly visible, avoid conflating a planetary nebula with a planet. Conceptual editorial scene only, not a data figure.

La portada conserva una tensión deliberada: contiene una ruta visual legible, pero los objetos aparecen en ramas y sistemas distintos. La escena no puede usarse para leer escala, duración, umbral de masa, frecuencia poblacional ni secuencia universal.

## Prompt de la portada de la Investigación 005

**Modo:** generación nueva, sin imagen de referencia. Archivo original de salida conservado por ImageGen; copia final inspeccionada en `assets/visuales/hero-formacion-sistema-solar.png`.

> Create an original wide 16:9 museum-quality editorial science illustration for a rigorous public Spanish-language research project about how we infer the formation of the Solar System. No text, no labels, no letters, no numbers, no arrows, no logos, no watermark. Visually distinguish but harmonize two archives of evidence. On the left and in the deep background, show a young star surrounded by an ALMA-like protoplanetary disk with realistic concentric rings, subtle gas glow and one modest asymmetric dust concentration; this is an analog system, so do not show our present planets inside it and do not imply every gap contains a planet. In the center, use a translucent conceptual transition into an early solar nebula around a protosun: gas and dust in a flattened rotating disk, a few diffuse pressure concentrations, chondrule-sized molten droplets, small irregular planetesimals, and a restrained stream of refractory grains moving outward; avoid a neat linear planet-formation sequence and avoid perfectly aligned finished planets. In the foreground, show the local fossil archive: a dark meteorite cut surface with pale calcium-aluminum-rich inclusions and round chondrules, a tiny polished grain under an ion microprobe, delicate paleomagnetic field traces, and a single Stardust-like cometary particle in aerogel. Make the two evidence archives visually distinct yet connected by elegant geometry and shared material motifs. Scientifically sober, realistic editorial natural-history aesthetic, deep navy and charcoal background, warm mineral amber, muted copper, cyan instrument accents, high detail but uncluttered, ample dark negative space near upper left for a future title. Conceptual scene only, not a data figure; do not present HL Tau or any observed disk as a photograph of the young Solar System; do not depict gaps as confirmed planets; no fantasy starships or dramatic explosions.

La portada comprime sistemas, escalas y épocas. El disco de la izquierda es un análogo genérico; el centro es una reconstrucción conceptual; el meteorito, la microsonda, la remanencia y Stardust representan mediciones actuales. La imagen no permite identificar planetas, estimar masa/radio, fechar eventos ni elegir mecanismos de transporte o formación planetesimal.

## Prompt de la portada de la Investigación 006

> Create a wide cinematic editorial science illustration, 16:9 landscape, for a Spanish public research project about how Earth accreted. One coherent scene with three depth layers, no panels and no text: in the distant background, the young inner Solar System as a dusty warm protoplanetary region with many irregular rocky planetary embryos on crossing paths, varied sizes, not a neat row of planets; in the middle distance, a grazing giant collision with realistic molten silicate spray, fragments and a partially differentiated body showing a subtle metallic core, explicitly a conceptual hit-and-run rather than two perfect spheres simply merging; in the foreground, a dark laboratory bench with a real-looking meteorite slice, a polished mantle rock, a tiny metal-silicate high-pressure experimental capsule, and the elegant ion optics silhouette of a modern mass spectrometer, connected visually by faint particle trails that evoke isotope ratios but contain no symbols or numbers. Scientific editorial realism, restrained palette of iron black, basalt gray, tungsten blue, molten amber and muted rust, high contrast, volumetric dust, precise textures, sophisticated museum-exhibition quality, ample dark negative space near top-left for an external title overlay. Avoid fantasy, astronauts, people, life, dinosaurs, modern Earth globe, visible Moon, labels, lettering, logos, equations, timelines, infographic arrows, perfectly circular orbital rings, duplicated planets, decorative constellations. This is conceptual art, not evidence and not a literal reconstruction.

La escena muestra un impacto oblicuo, un cuerpo diferenciado y análisis moderno para hacer visible la cadena de inferencia. No fija el impactor lunar, la geometría real, la masa perdida, el número de choques, la profundidad del océano de magma ni una edad.

## Reglas de mantenimiento

- conservar SVG con `title` y `desc` accesibles;
- no copiar figuras de publicaciones sin licencia y atribución verificadas;
- registrar fecha, herramienta, prompt o código y transformaciones;
- distinguir `conceptual`, `reconstrucción`, `dato`, `mapa` y `diagrama`;
- si cambia un claim, revisar también todos los visuales que lo resuman.
