# Registro de visuales

Los visuales explicativos son una capa editorial; nunca sustituyen datos, figuras primarias ni registros de evidencia. Cuando una pieza sea conceptual, su leyenda debe decirlo. Cuando represente relaciones científicas, debe poder reconstruirse desde código o texto y enlazar los claims pertinentes.

## Prompt de la portada

**Modo:** generación nueva, sin imagen de referencia.

> Create an original wide 16:9 cinematic editorial illustration for an open Spanish-language scientific research project titled conceptually “How do we know what we know? Deep history of Earth, life, and humanity.” No text, no labels, no logos, no watermark. One continuous left-to-right visual journey with smooth transitions: faint cosmic microwave background texture and cosmic web; stellar nucleosynthesis and a forming protoplanetary disk; a luminous molten early Earth with a small young Moon; cooling crust with a microscopic zircon crystal; an ancient ocean with subtle microbial mats; layered fossils and branching life; distant early human silhouettes observing the sky; the faint geometry of an early city at the far right. Scientifically grounded rather than fantasy, elegant dark indigo and charcoal background, mineral gold and cyan highlights, restrained color, high-detail natural-history museum aesthetic, dramatic but sober, ample dark negative space near upper center for a title to be added later. Do not show dinosaurs beside humans, do not imply literal scale or simultaneity, no religious symbols, no clocks, no equations, no typography.

La imagen se conserva como una metáfora temporal continua. Los elementos vecinos pertenecen a épocas distintas; esa compresión se declara para impedir una lectura literal.

## Prompt de la portada de la Investigación 003

**Modo:** generación nueva, sin imagen de referencia.

> Wide 16:9 editorial science illustration for a rigorous public research project about the origin of the chemical elements. A coherent left-to-right cosmic history in one museum-quality scene: at far left the hot early universe cooling into simple hydrogen and helium nuclei; then a luminous main-sequence star with a subtle cutaway showing nested fusion regions; next a red AGB giant releasing glowing dust and microscopic presolar grains; then an asymmetric core-collapse supernova with faint gamma-ray arcs; then two neutron stars merging into a blue-to-red kilonova; in the foreground a dark meteorite fragment, a single silicon-carbide grain under a laboratory ion probe, and a glass spectrograph prism splitting starlight into fine spectral lines. Sober deep navy, charcoal, amber, cyan and muted magenta palette; realistic scientific editorial illustration, intricate but uncluttered, strong visual flow, generous negative space near the upper left for a future title. No text, no letters, no numbers, no periodic table, no labels, no watermark, no logos. Conceptual scene only; avoid implying that any one source exclusively creates an element.

La portada es conceptual. El aparente orden espacial solo guía lectura; no afirma simultaneidad, escala, anatomía estelar exacta ni genealogía exclusiva de núcleos.

## Prompt de la portada de la Investigación 004

**Modo:** generación nueva, sin imagen de referencia. Archivo original de salida conservado por ImageGen; copia final en `assets/visuales/hero-evolucion-estelar.png`.

> Create an original wide 16:9 museum-quality editorial science illustration for a rigorous public Spanish-language research project about how we reconstruct stellar birth, evolution, and death. No text, no labels, no letters, no numbers, no arrows, no logos, no watermark. Compose a visually coherent but clearly branching scene, not one universal linear lifecycle: at left a cold dark molecular cloud with an embedded protostar, a resolved circumstellar disk, and a narrow bipolar jet; toward center a stable sun-like star and a diverse coeval star cluster; from there the scene branches. One branch shows a swollen red giant shedding a luminous but delicate shell around a compact white dwarf. A separate branch shows a massive red supergiant and an asymmetric core-collapse supernova leading to a compact rotating neutron star with subtle lighthouse-like beams. In the far background, a massive blue companion orbits an invisible stellar-mass black-hole candidate with a restrained accretion glow, avoiding fantasy depictions of the event horizon. In the foreground integrate observational instruments as visual motifs: a parallax baseline against background stars, a glass spectrograph splitting starlight, an eclipsing binary light curve suggested only as luminous geometry without written symbols, and a sparse color–magnitude population pattern rendered as points rather than a labeled chart. Scientifically sober, deep navy and charcoal, cold molecular blues, amber stellar light, muted crimson supernova, cyan instrument accents, high detail, elegant natural-history museum aesthetic, ample dark negative space near upper left for a future title. Avoid showing exact mass thresholds, avoid implying all stars pass through every object, avoid presenting the black hole as directly visible, avoid conflating a planetary nebula with a planet. Conceptual editorial scene only, not a data figure.

La portada conserva una tensión deliberada: contiene una ruta visual legible, pero los objetos aparecen en ramas y sistemas distintos. La escena no puede usarse para leer escala, duración, umbral de masa, frecuencia poblacional ni secuencia universal.

## Reglas de mantenimiento

- conservar SVG con `title` y `desc` accesibles;
- no copiar figuras de publicaciones sin licencia y atribución verificadas;
- registrar fecha, herramienta, prompt o código y transformaciones;
- distinguir `conceptual`, `reconstrucción`, `dato`, `mapa` y `diagrama`;
- si cambia un claim, revisar también todos los visuales que lo resuman.
